Quiz instructions

Cadences

Listen to two chords and identify how they return to the home chord.

Listen to the progression

Press Play and listen to how the first chord moves into the final chord. You can listen again whenever you need to.

Choose the cadence

Choose Authentic, Plagal, Minor Plagal or Sub-Plagal. Listen for the type of chord that leads into the home chord.

Check and submit

You can change your choice before you submit. When you are ready, press Submit Answer.

Chord 15Dominant 7th
Chord 21Major
Example answer

Authentic

In F, C dominant seventh moves to F major. This is an Authentic cadence. Choose Authentic.

What changes in this quiz?

A cadence is a short chord movement that sounds like an ending. These examples use all twelve keys and finish on the home major chord. Authentic moves from 5 dominant seventh to 1; Plagal from 4 major to 1; Minor Plagal from 4 minor to 1; Sub-Plagal from 2 minor to 1. Some versions add a seventh to 4 major or 2 minor, or a sixth to 4 minor. The home chord may include an added ninth or major seventh. The notes can be rearranged, but the cadence name stays the same.

Diatonic Progression #1

Use the opening reference to hear the key, then enter the four chord numbers in order.

Listen to the progression

Press Play. First you hear the key’s home major chord, then its single home note. After these references, listen to the four progression chords. You can listen again whenever you need to.

Build your answer

For each of the four answer slots, choose the chord number from 1 to 7. The chord type is filled in for you.

Check and submit

Check the chords from left to right. You can select a chord slot to change it before you submit. When you are ready, press Submit Answer.

Before the answer: home major chord → home note.

Chord 12Minor
Chord 25Major
Chord 31Major
Chord 44Major

This example follows 2 → 5 → 1 → 4. Choose these four numbers in order; each chord type is provided automatically.

What changes in this quiz?

Diatonic means the chords use notes from the same major scale. This quiz uses all twelve keys. The home chord is called 1, and the other chord numbers come from their place in that scale. In F, the numbers 1 to 7 start on F, G, A, B♭, C, D and E. The chord types are 1 Major, 2 Minor, 3 Minor, 4 Major, 5 Major, 6 Minor and 7 Diminished. The opening home chord and single note help you hear the key; do not enter them in your answer. The chord notes can be rearranged, so the lowest note is not always the note the chord is named after.

Progression Build #1

Listen to four chords, then choose a number and a chord type for each one.

Listen to the progression

Press Play. First hear the key’s home major chord, then its single home note. The four chords you need to identify follow those references. You can listen again whenever you need to.

Build your answer

Select each chord slot and choose both its number and its type. The type is not fixed by the number in this quiz.

Check and submit

Check the chords from left to right. You can select a chord slot to change it before you submit. When you are ready, press Submit Answer.

Before the answer: home major chord → home note.

Chord 12Minor
Chord 23Major
Chord 36Minor
Chord 45Major

This visual example shows four complete choices. The 3 is Major in this example, even though it would usually be Minor within the home major scale. Choose the type you hear.

What changes in this quiz?

This quiz uses all twelve keys and includes chords with notes outside the home major scale. That means the same chord number can have a different type. Choose Major, Minor, Diminished or Sus4. The ♭7 number means a chord built one keyboard step below the scale’s seventh note. A Sus4 chord leads into a major chord on the same number. The opening home chord and single home note are references only; enter the four chords that follow. The chord notes stay close together and can be rearranged.

Progression Recognition #1

You will hear three chords in a 2–5–1 sequence. Choose the type of each chord.

Listen to the progression

Press Play and listen to the first, second and final chord in order. You can listen again whenever you need to.

Build your answer

The three chord numbers are already set to 2, 5 and 1. Select each slot and choose its chord type from the available options.

Check and submit

Check the chords from left to right. You can select a chord slot to change it before you submit. When you are ready, press Submit Answer.

Chord 12Minor 7th
Chord 25Dominant 7th
Chord 31Major 7th

In this example, choose Minor 7th for 2, Dominant 7th for 5, and Major 7th for 1.

What changes in this quiz?

This quiz uses the key of F. The 2 and 1 chords have their root at the bottom. The 5 chord uses second inversion, with its fifth at the bottom. For 2, choose Minor 7th, Minor 7(♭5), Dominant 7th or Minor 6. For 5, choose Dominant 7th, Minor 7th or Minor 7(♭5). For 1, choose Dominant 7th, Minor 7th, Major 7th, Major 6 or Minor 6. Your answer is the three chord types, not an inversion name.

Quiz instructions

2-5-1 chord degree

You will hear 2–5–1 with added chord notes. Choose each chord type and all its extensions.

Listen to the progression

Press Play and listen to all three chords in order. You can listen again whenever you need to.

Build your answer

The numbers 2, 5 and 1 are given. For each slot, choose its chord type, then select every added extension present. Each chord needs at least one extension.

Check and submit

Check the chords from left to right. You can select a chord slot to change it before you submit. When you are ready, press Submit Answer.

Chord 12Minor 7th9
Chord 25Dominant 7th9, ♯11, 13
Chord 31Major 7th9

Choose Minor 7th with 9 for 2; Dominant 7th with 9, ♯11 and 13 for 5; and Major 7th with 9 for 1. Include every extension shown, not just the highest one.

What changes in this quiz?

This quiz uses all twelve keys. All chords have their root, the note they are named after, at the bottom. Choose the chord type first; the available extension buttons depend on that choice. A seventh or sixth is already included in the type name, so select only the additional extensions. For example, Minor 7th with 9 means Minor 9th. More than one extension can be needed, and their selection order does not matter.

Chordal Progressions

Follow the five-chord sequence and choose the type of each chord in order.

Listen to the progression

Press Play and listen to all five chord sounds from beginning to end. You can listen again whenever you need to.

Build your answer

Select each of the five slots and choose its chord type. This quiz asks for the type only; there is no chord-number selection.

Check and submit

Check the chords from left to right. You can select a chord slot to change it before you submit. When you are ready, press Submit Answer.

Chord 1NoneMajor
Chord 2NoneMinor 7th
Chord 3NoneDominant 7th
Chord 4NoneMajor 7th
Chord 5NoneMinor

This visual example has five answers, one for each chord. Choose the types from left to right, without entering chord numbers.

What changes in this quiz?

This quiz uses five-chord examples from the Chordal Melodies and Chordal Bass Lines pool, across all twelve keys. Here, your task is to identify each whole chord, rather than its highest or lowest note. The choices include major, minor, diminished and augmented triads; the five standard seventh-chord types; and the six secondary seventh-chord types. Rearranging the chord notes does not change its type.
