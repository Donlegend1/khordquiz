01 / Understand the task

Chord Tone
Recognition

Hear a chord. Then hear one note on its own.
Can you tell what that note does inside the chord?

What you are practising

You are learning to recognise the root, third, fifth and seventh of a chord by ear. These are the four roles a note can have in this quiz.

Why it helps

You can hear how a melody note fits a chord, follow bass lines more confidently and recognise chord sounds even when their notes are rearranged.

03 / Understand inversions

Different positions.
The same four roles.

An inversion rearranges the chord notes so a different note sits at the bottom. The root keeps its name and role.

1 does not mean “the lowest note.”

If the chord is A–C–E–F and the single note is F, the answer is still 1. A is at the bottom, but the chord is named after F.

The chord type matters too

A lowered or raised note keeps its role. Read the chord type in the question before listening.

F major seventh

F · A · C · E

A is the third → choose 3.
E is the seventh → choose 7.

F minor seventh

F · A♭ · C · E♭

A♭ is still the third → choose 3.
E♭ is still the seventh → choose 7.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

04 / Your listening routine

One chord.
One note. One choice.

Read the question

Notice the chord type that is given, such as Major 7th or Minor 7th.

Hear the whole chord

Press Play. Get a sense of the full sound before focusing on the single note.

Follow the note on its own

Listen for where it belongs inside the chord. Replay the audio if you need to.

Choose and submit

Select 1, 3, 5 or 7. You can change your choice before pressing Submit Answer.
