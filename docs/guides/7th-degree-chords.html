Quiz instructions

7th Degree Chords · Level 1

You will hear four notes played together. Choose the seventh-chord type that matches the sound.

Listen to the chord

Press Play to hear all four notes together. Listen to the sound they make as a group. You can listen again whenever you need to.

Choose the chord type

Choose Diminished 7th, Dominant 7th, Minor 7(♭5), Major 7th, Minor 7th. These names describe the different sounds the four notes can make together.

Submit your answer

When you are ready, press Submit Answer. You can change your choice before you submit.

What changes in this quiz?

A seventh chord has four notes: a three-note chord with a seventh added. For example, F major uses F, A and C; adding E makes F major seventh. “Chord quality” means the chord type. This lesson uses chords built on F. The notes use a close layout, without spreading individual notes into a different high or low range. Choose the chord type only; there is no separate inversion answer. Minor 7(♭5) means minor seventh with a lowered fifth, also called half-diminished.

What changes in this quiz?

A seventh chord has four notes: a three-note chord with a seventh added. For example, F major uses F, A and C; adding E makes F major seventh. “Chord quality” means the chord type. This lesson uses chords built on F. The notes use a close layout, without spreading individual notes into a different high or low range. In a close chord, Root Position has the root at the bottom, 1st Inversion has the third, 2nd Inversion has the fifth, and 3rd Inversion has the seventh. The root is the note the chord is named after; the third, fifth and seventh are its other notes. Minor 7(♭5) means minor seventh with a lowered fifth, also called half-diminished.
