13th Degree Chords · Level 1

You will hear a chord with several notes played together, including a thirteenth. Choose the chord name that matches its sound.

Listen to the whole chord

Press Play and listen to the notes together. Focus on the overall sound rather than trying to name every note. You can listen again whenever you need to.

Choose the chord type

Select Major 13(♯11), Minor 13th, Dominant 13(♯11) or Dominant 13(♭9,♯11). Listen for the difference between the four chord sounds.

Submit your answer

When you are ready, press Submit Answer. You can change your choice before you submit.

Example answer

Minor 13th

This visual example is F minor thirteenth. Choose Minor 13th. During the quiz, choose the chord type you hear, even when the notes are arranged differently.

What changes in this quiz?

These chords add a thirteenth to a chord that also contains a seventh, ninth and eleventh or raised eleventh. For example, F minor thirteenth uses F, A♭, C, E♭, G, B♭ and D. The added D is the thirteenth. In the answer names, ♯11 means a raised eleventh and ♭9 means a lowered ninth. Raising or lowering a note means moving one keyboard step, counting both white and black keys. This lesson uses chords built on F. The root, the note the chord is named after, stays at the bottom. Choose only the chord type; there is no separate inversion answer.
