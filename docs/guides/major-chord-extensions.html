Major Chord Extensions · Level 1

The chord type is Major. Listen and select all the added note numbers that make up the chord you hear.

Listen to the whole chord

Press Play and listen to how the extra notes change the major chord. You can listen again whenever you need to.

Select every note number you hear

Choose all the numbers that belong to the chord, such as 6, 7, 9 or ♯11. More than one answer can be needed. Tap a selected number again to remove it.

Check and submit

Check all your selected numbers before pressing Submit Answer. Include 7 whenever the chord contains a seventh. For example, Major 9(♯11) needs 7, 9 and ♯11.

F, A and C make the major chord. G adds 9 and B adds ♯11, so select 9 and ♯11.

What changes in this quiz?

A major chord starts with three notes. Added notes give it a different sound; their numbers are what you select in this quiz. The ♭ sign means a lowered note and ♯ means a raised note, by one keyboard step, counting white and black keys. Group 1 uses a major three-note chord with a sixth, seventh or one or two added notes. This lesson uses chords built on F. The chords use their basic arrangement, with no doubled or shifted notes. Select the notes present, not just the largest number in the chord name. 6 and 13 can refer to the same note name in different positions: this quiz uses 6 for the sixth-chord group and 13 for extensions above a seventh.
