KHORDQUIZ · 01 · FOUNDATIONS

What does “stay in C” mean?

C remains the place where the phrase feels settled—even when another chord briefly gets our attention.

The familiar chords of C major

DegreeTriadSeventh chord
1 · CC majorCmaj7
2 · DD minorDm7
3 · EE minorEm7
4 · FF majorFmaj7
5 · GG majorG7
6 · AA minorAm7
7 · BB diminishedBm7♭5

Three useful words

Diatonic

Every note of the chord belongs to C major. Dm7 contains D–F–A–C, so it is diatonic.

Non-diatonic / chromatic

At least one chord note is outside C major. A7 contains A–C♯–E–G. C♯ is outside the scale, so A7 is non-diatonic.

Tonal centre

The home of the whole phrase. A chord can briefly point toward D minor while the overall phrase still returns naturally to C.

For the proposed five-chord exercises, a useful starting constraint is at least three diatonic chord events and no more than two chromatic events. This is a composition guideline, not a universal law of music. It still needs final approval for generation.

KHORDQUIZ · 02 · VOICE LEADING

Move the notes, not the whole shape.

Each note is a voice. Keep shared notes where possible and move the others by small distances.

D major → G major

A3→B3
F♯3→G3
D3staysD3

Top row = melody. Bottom row = bass. Play each column together.

A major → D minor

E4→F4
C♯4→D4
A3staysA3

Top row = melody. Bottom row = bass. Play each column together.

What is happening?

ConnectionSmall movements
D → GD stays. F♯ rises to G. A rises to B. G major is in second inversion because D is its lowest note.
A → DmA stays. C♯ rises to D. E rises to F. D minor is in second inversion because A is its lowest note.

Root, bass and melody are different

The root names the chord. The bass is its lowest sounding note. The melody is its highest sounding note. Changing inversion changes the bass; it does not dictate the melody.

What to avoid in these exercises

Do not move to a distant register merely to put every chord in root position. Root-position movement is not inherently wrong; this exercise deliberately favours nearby voicings so the phrase feels connected.

Try it

Play the left column as one block chord, then the right column. Listen to the top row as a tiny melody. Say the bass note separately. Octave numbers locate the actual keys: C4 is middle C; larger numbers sound higher.

Listen for the journey

  1. C establishes home. The familiar sound gives the phrase a starting point.
  2. A7 adds tension. Its C♯ points toward D. This is an applied, or secondary, dominant approach.
  3. Dm7 receives the approach. We have arrived at a chord inside C major, not established a new home key.
  4. G7 leads home. The phrase prepares its return to C.
  5. Cmaj7 settles the phrase with colour. Its B is the major seventh, not an added wrong note.

For the chordal-melody quiz

Play all notes of a chord together. The answer follows the highest note of each block: C4 → E4 → C4 → B3 → B3. We are following the top line, not the roots C–A–D–G–C.

The A7 and G7 voicings are inversions. Their roots are still present. These particular voicings illustrate connected movement; they are not the only good choices.

Each voice rises by one or two semitones. This creates a clear approach into Dm7 while preserving a compact register.

The agreed Fm6 / Dm7♭5 naming choice

Use Dm7♭5 consistently

Fm6 and Dm7♭5 share the pitch classes F–A♭–C–D. The owner has chosen the label Dm7♭5 throughout these new rules. Inversions are allowed; a voicing with F at the bottom is Dm7♭5 in first inversion under this convention.

Replacing a label does not require moving the same four notes to a distant register. In general musical analysis, context can favour a different root interpretation. This is an explicit project convention.

Do not confuse these

Am7♭5 = A–C–E♭–G. Dm7♭5 = D–F–A♭–C. They are different pitch collections and are not interchangeable names.

Two uses of “secondary”

The app’s “secondary seventh chords” is a category of chord qualities. A secondary dominant is a harmonic function: for example A7 approaching Dm. These terms do not mean the same thing.

The dictionary explains available names; it does not mean every quality already has approved passing rules. Rootless A9 is taught through C♯m7♭5. No suspended chords or separate minor-sixth labels are included in the new convention.

Example in C: C → B♭maj7 → F → G7 → C. B♭maj7 approaches IV; the phrase then returns home through G7–C.

Keep C in the frame

A chord pair alone does not establish a whole key. Place it within a phrase that clearly belongs to C. Avoid repeating a local arrival so much that it becomes a new home.

Beginner practice

Start with D–G and E–Am. Find a common note, hold it where possible, then move the remaining voices to the nearest usable chord notes. Add a short diatonic route back to C.

B♭7 is a dominant-seventh quality and appears on the next page, even though it was originally mentioned alongside major-seventh examples.

Function and voice leading are different explanations

E7 → Am is an applied-dominant resolution. A more unusual route such as B7 → F needs a convincing note-by-note explanation in its actual voicing. Do not label every owner-specified route a standard dominant resolution.

Earlier examples also retained

B♭7 → C or Cmaj7 is retained as a borrowed approach to tonic; B♭7 → Am7 is another owner-specified connection. G7 → C remains the ordinary dominant return. Keep the actual voices close and judge each route within the whole phrase.

All former Fm6 destinations have been renamed Dm7♭5 under the project convention. These may be voice-leading or reharmonization choices rather than textbook cadence labels.

Fully diminished: dim7

The owner’s general device is a dim7 rooted a semitone below the next chord’s root. Example: C♯dim7 → Dm7. Confirm its actual voice leading; the root relationship alone is not a guarantee.

Use the effect sparingly

The owner’s limit is one fully diminished seventh chord event per sequence. This counts dim7, not dimMaj7. A sequence containing CdimMaj7 → Cdim7 uses one fully diminished event.

Targets in the general rule may be major/minor triads, maj7, min7, dom7 or m7♭5. No target automatically changes the home key; the complete phrase still needs a C-major frame.

KHORDQUIZ · 10 · PRACTICE WORKSHOP

Build a phrase you can explain.

Start simple. Make the connection sound natural before adding another colour.

  1. Choose a destination inside C major. Start with Dm, F or G. Know where your phrase is going.
  2. Choose one approach from the palette. Try A7 before Dm7, or Fm before C. Read the full connection, not just one chord name.
  3. Place the voices close together. Keep shared notes where practical. Listen to bass and top line separately.
  4. Complete the five-chord phrase. Give the approach enough context for C to remain home. A final C or Cmaj7 is a useful teaching constraint, not a law for every musical phrase.
  5. Play all five blocks. Can you sing the highest notes? Does the final arrival feel connected to what came before?
  6. Check the actual pitches. The name, bass/inversion and highest note must agree with the keys being played.

Questions for the learner

Beginner

In A–Dm, which note stays? Which chord contains C♯? In the worked five-chord phrase on page 4, sing only the top notes.

Intermediate

Explain why A7 is outside C major but Dm7 is inside it. Play C♯m7♭5 → Dm7. Then play Dm7♭5 → C using nearby voicings.

Advanced

Voice B♭dimMaj7 → Fmaj7 exactly as shown, then find another compact inversion pair. Explain each voice movement without pretending the connection is a standard V–I.

Answer hints: A is held in the page-3 A–Dm example. C♯ belongs to A major/A7, not to the diatonic C-major scale. Standard applied-dominant language fits A7–Dm; uncommon colour routes need their own voice-leading explanation.

Generation proposals—not yet a final brief

Five chords per new sequence; a mostly diatonic C-major frame; compact voicings; no more than one dim7 event; no suspended chords; and the agreed Dm7♭5 naming. The earlier suggestions of at least three diatonic events, a final C/Cmaj7 and the exact register/leap limits still need final approval against the expanded palette.
